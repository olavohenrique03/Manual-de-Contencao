REPTILES

Common rule

Reptiles are ectothermic, and their metabolic activity depends on environmental temperature. They do not have a diaphragm, and ventilation occurs through muscular movements that vary among groups. During restraint, keep the body supported, avoid compressing the coelomic cavity, and never use the limbs, tail, or neck as the only points of support. Work in a safe environment, at a temperature appropriate for the species, and for the shortest time possible.

Applies to all records in this group:

  • Procedures requiring restraint: general physical examination, assessment of the integument, carapace and plastron, inspection of the oral cavity, limbs and joints, medication administration, biological sample collection, assessment of injuries, wound care, and diagnostic procedures when indicated.
  • PPE and hygiene: nitrile gloves, surgical scrubs or a clinical coat, and materials for hygiene and disinfection. Additional equipment should be used according to the size and defensive mechanisms of the species.
  • Preparation: observe posture, breathing pattern, activity, and possible signs of pain or weakness while the animal is still in its enclosure. Organize materials and the team in advance, keeping the environment quiet, safe, and thermally appropriate.
  • Monitoring: observe breathing, resistance, behavior, and body stability throughout restraint. After release, monitor recovery of posture, locomotion, and normal activity. For prolonged, invasive procedures or procedures incompatible with physical restraint, the veterinarian should assess the need for chemical restraint.
