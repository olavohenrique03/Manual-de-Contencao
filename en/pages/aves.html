BIRDS

Common rule

Birds do not have a diaphragm and breathe through movement of the sternum and ribs. Never compress the chest or coelomic cavity, keep the bird in an anatomical position (avoid prolonged dorsal recumbency), and keep handling as brief as possible.

Applies to all records in this group:

  • Procedures requiring restraint: general physical examination, inspection of the beak and oral cavity, assessment of wings, feathers, and pelvic limbs, medication administration, biological sample collection, assessment of injuries and wound care, nail trimming, and beak maintenance when indicated.
  • PPE and hygiene: nitrile gloves, surgical scrubs or a clinical coat, and materials for hygiene and disinfection. Exceptions are indicated in the species record.
  • Preparation: a quiet environment with low light when appropriate, without obstacles that may cause collisions; plan control of the head, wings, and feet before capture.
  • Monitoring: observe breathing (including open-mouth breathing), mucous membranes, posture, and response to stimuli. After release, monitor balance, activity, and breathing until the bird returns to its usual pattern.
