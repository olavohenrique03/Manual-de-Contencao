Common rule

Protect the gills and mucus layer. Use moistened nitrile gloves, wet surfaces, and nothing dry or abrasive. Never use the opercula, fins, or tail as the only point of support, and keep the fish out of the water for the shortest time possible. If the procedure can be performed submerged, keep it submerged.

Applies to all records in this group:

  • Procedures requiring restraint: general physical examination, assessment of the integument and scales, inspection of fins and opercula, assessment of external lesions, collection of superficial samples, medication, and brief therapeutic procedures.
  • Before restraint: observe the fish while it is still in the water (swimming, activity, body position, opercular movements, and response to approach). Prepare all materials in advance, with water parameters compatible with the original environment, to avoid repeated captures and chasing.
  • PPE and hygiene: moistened nitrile gloves, surgical scrubs or a clinical coat, and materials for hygiene and cleaning.
  • At the end: return the fish to the water and observe posture, swimming, frequency and regularity of opercular movements, and response to stimuli. Check for injuries to the integument and fins and monitor recovery until normal behavior returns.
