About Wild Guide

Wild Guide is an illustrated technical-scientific manual created from an Undergraduate Research project at Universidade Anhembi Morumbi. It brings together, in one place, information that is currently scattered across books, articles, and technical documents, and is intended for Veterinary Medicine students and professionals.

Physical restraint of wild and exotic animals kept as pets requires knowledge of the anatomical, physiological, and behavioral particularities of each species. When performed incorrectly, restraint can cause injuries, intensify stress, and compromise the safety of both the patient and the handler. For this reason, the manual relates each technique to the animal’s biological characteristics and combines practical guidance, scientific background, and visual resources (photographs and demonstration videos on the digital platform) for quick and educational consultation.

The objective is to support academic training and clinical practice with safe methods that reduce stress and respect animal welfare.

How to use this manual

The content is organized into five taxonomic groups: mammals, birds, reptiles, fish, and amphibians. Each group begins with a common rule that applies to all of its species and is not repeated in the individual records. Each species record includes:

  • About the species classification, anatomy, behavior, and risks that affect the technique.
  • Before restraint what to observe and prepare before the first contact.
  • Primary technique indication and step-by-step procedure, with the anatomical and physiological rationale.
  • Alternatives other techniques and when to use them.
  • Never do practices contraindicated for the species.
  • Stop if signs indicating that the technique should be changed or stopped.
  • Materials what should be prepared, in addition to what is already listed in the group’s common rule.

The primary technique is intended for educational purposes and is not necessarily the most appropriate option for every situation. Always adapt the choice to the clinical condition, behavior, and size of each patient.

Important notice

This material is intended for educational purposes and does not replace supervised practical training, individual patient assessment, or the clinical judgment of the veterinarian. The images and videos on the platform were produced with approval from CEUA-UAM (Protocol No. 24/2026).

General principles and limits of application

Five principles guide every restraint technique described in this manual:

  1. Use the least restraint necessary. Use enough control for the procedure without unnecessary force.
  2. Plan before touching. Assess the clinical condition, behavior, and breathing pattern; prepare the environment and materials; define each person’s role.
  3. Monitor continuously. Observe breathing, mucous membranes, and behavior throughout the restraint.
  4. Know when to stop. Changing the technique or stopping is part of safe handling. If restraint fails, reorganize instead of applying more force.
  5. Keep it brief. Short handling periods reduce stress and risk.

Avoid compression that interferes with ventilation, positions incompatible with the anatomy of the species, and maneuvers that may cause trauma. Also consider team safety, especially with species that have defensive mechanisms capable of causing injury. Venomous or high-risk animals require trained professionals, appropriate equipment, and specific protocols.

When physical restraint does not provide adequate safety conditions, the veterinarian responsible should consider other strategies, such as chemical restraint.
